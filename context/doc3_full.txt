Abstract
Independent pharmacies in Colombia face increasing competitive pressure from the expansion of large pharmaceutical chains equipped with advanced technology management systems. In this context, this article proposes the design of a CRM (Customer Relationship Management) software prototype specialized for independent pharmacies, integrating inventory management with expiration date control, sales and promotion automation, and customer loyalty features. From a business management perspective applied to software development, the work combines a theoretical bibliographic review with the design of a functional prototype. A CRM solution adapted to the operational particularities of independent pharmacies can increase operational efficiency, reduce losses from product expiration, and improve customer retention. The article concludes that there is a significant technological gap in this segment of the Colombian market and that the development of accessible, specialized tools represents a concrete software engineering innovation opportunity.
Keywords: CRM, independent pharmacies, inventory management, sales automation, customer loyalty, software, Colombia.
1. Introduction
1.1 Problem Statement
The pharmaceutical sector in Colombia comprises an extensive network of establishments including both large chains and independent drugstores and pharmacies. According to the Colombian Ministry of Health and Social Protection, the country has more than 20,000 registered pharmaceutical establishments, a significant proportion of which are family-owned or micro-enterprises. These establishments play a fundamental role in ensuring access to medications, particularly in intermediate municipalities and peri-urban areas where large chains have no presence.
This market segment is of vital importance to the Colombian healthcare system, as it guarantees the dispensing of medicines to populations that would otherwise have limited access. Paradoxically, however, these are also the establishments with the greatest operational and competitive fragility, as they must face pressure from national chains such as La Rebaja, Drogas La EconomÃ­a, and Farmatodo, which rely on sophisticated technological infrastructures, automated management systems, and data-driven loyalty strategies.
The central problem motivating this research lies in the technological gap separating independent pharmacies from their larger-scale competitors. Most of these establishments operate with rudimentary management systemsâ€”or even paper-based recordsâ€”which hampers precise inventory control, generates economic losses due to product expiration, prevents the automation of sales and promotional processes, and severely limits their capacity to build long-term customer relationships.
This situation constitutes a business management problem with a profound technological dimension: the absence of software tools that are accessible, affordable, and adapted to the operational realities of independent Colombian pharmacies. This is not merely a technical limitation but a structural barrier that condemns these businesses to compete under unequal conditions, compromising their sustainability and, ultimately, the populationâ€™s access to decentralized pharmaceutical services.
1.2 Theoretical Framework
This article is grounded in the theoretical perspective of information technology-based business management, specifically in the CRM (Customer Relationship Management) paradigm. This approach conceives the customer relationship as the central strategic asset of any organization and posits that technology must serve the construction and management of those relationships (Kotler & Keller, 2016). From this perspective, CRM is not merely software but a management philosophy that aligns processes, people, and technology around the customer.
The fundamental principles of CRM theory applied in this work are articulated around three axes or systematization categories. The first is intelligent inventory management, understood as a systemâ€™s capacity to monitor stock levels in real time, alert users to impending expiration dates, and generate rotation reports to support purchasing decisions. This principle connects directly with Supply Chain Management theory, which emphasizes the importance of reducing waste and optimizing product flows (Chopra & Meindl, 2016).
The second axis is sales and promotion automation, grounded in the concept of Sales Force Automation (SFA), which proposes the use of technological tools to standardize, streamline, and enhance commercial processes (Buttle, 2009). In the context of an independent pharmacy, this translates into functionalities such as automatic restocking alerts, the scheduling of seasonal or near-expiry discounts, and the systematic recording of each customerâ€™s purchase history.
The third axis is customer loyalty, supported by Berryâ€™s (1983) relational marketing theory and subsequently developed by Zeithaml, Berry & Parasuraman (1996), which posits that retaining an existing customer is significantly more profitable than acquiring a new one. In the pharmaceutical sector, customer loyalty also carries a therapeutic adherence dimension: when a pharmacy is familiar with a patientâ€™s medication history, it can generate restocking reminders, detect potential interactions, and build a trust-based relationship that extends beyond the commercial transaction.

2. Methodology
This work adopts a mixed methodological approach combining two complementary strategies. First, a systematic bibliographic review of the literature on CRM, pharmaceutical inventory management, and sales automation in small businesses was conducted, consulting academic databases such as Scopus, Google Scholar, and Colombian institutional repositories. This review made it possible to establish the state of the art, identify existing gaps in the literature, and ground the prototype requirements.
Second, the work employs prototype design as a development strategy. Following the User-Centered Design (UCD) methodology, a requirements analysis was conducted based on the literature on the needs of independent Colombian pharmacies, from which the functional modules of the CRM were defined. The prototype was designed using wireframing and data modeling tools, with the aim of producing a functional representation that can be assessed and validated in future research.
The prototype design followed the phases of the Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC): requirements analysis, architecture design, database modeling, and interface design. For the system architecture, an MVC (Model-View-Controller) pattern was adopted, with a relational database (PostgreSQL) and a responsive web interface accessible from mobile devices, taking into account the variable connectivity conditions of the Colombian context.

3.2 Proposed CRM Prototype Architecture
The proposed CRM software prototype adopts a three-layer architecture based on the MVC pattern. The presentation layer consists of a responsive web interface developed with React.js, ensuring accessibility from mobile devices and tablets commonly used in small Colombian businesses. The business logic layer is implemented in Node.js with Express, managing the business rules related to expiration alerts, loyalty point calculations, and promotion automation. The data layer uses PostgreSQL as the relational database management system, with a normalized structure that enables efficient management of inventory, customers, sales, and campaigns.
The system operates under a low-cost SaaS (Software as a Service) model, with offline synchronization to ensure operational continuity in contexts of limited connectivityâ€”a frequent condition in intermediate Colombian municipalities. Authentication and access control is role-based (administrator, cashier, supervisor), allowing the system to adapt to varying staff structures. The central data model articulates four primary entities: Product, Customer, Sale, and Campaign, linked through foreign keys that ensure referential integrity.
4. Discussion
The results of this work confirm and extend the findings of previous research on the technological gap in the independent Colombian pharmaceutical sector (Ruiz & MartÃ­nez, 2021; Vargas, 2022). The proposal of an integrated CRM represents a step forward from the fragmented systems previously explored in the literature, by bringing together inventory management, sales, and customer relationship processes within a single platform.
A relevant finding of this work is the centrality of the expiration control module as a competitive differentiator. Unlike generic CRMs available on the market (Salesforce, HubSpot, Zoho CRM), which do not incorporate specialized logic for managing pharmaceutical product expiration dates, the proposed prototype integrates this functionality natively, connecting it directly with the promotion automation module. This allows the system not only to alert users about near-expiry products but also to automatically activate progressive discounts to accelerate turnover and reduce economic losses.
The customer loyalty dimension acquires particular relevance in the context of the independent Colombian pharmacy. While large chains compete primarily on price and location, independent pharmacies have the potential advantage of personalized service and customer knowledge. The proposed loyalty module leverages this advantage by systematizing it: recording each customerâ€™s medication history makes it possible to generate restocking reminders that add value beyond the commercial transaction, building a trust-based relationship that chains cannot easily replicate.
An important limitation of this work is that the proposed prototype has not been validated through real-world implementation. This is a fundamental stage that future research must address, ideally through a case study at an independent Colombian pharmacy that would allow for assessment of system usability, staff adoption, and measurable impact on operational indicators. Additionally, it will be necessary to analyze the regulatory implications of managing personal health data under the framework of Law 1581 of 2012 (Habeas Data) and INVIMA regulations.
5. Conclusions
This article has demonstrated that independent pharmacies in Colombia face a significant technological gap that limits their competitive capacity vis-Ã -vis large pharmaceutical chains. This gap is not primarily the result of economic constraints but of the absence of software solutions specifically designed for the operational, scale, and connectivity conditions of these establishments.
The proposed CRM software prototype represents a concrete response to this need by integrating, within a single accessible platform, the modules for inventory management with expiration control, sales and promotion automation, and customer loyalty. The adoption of a lightweight SaaS architecture with offline capability constitutes a particularly pertinent design decision for the Colombian context, where connectivity conditions in intermediate municipalities are variable.
From a software engineering perspective, this work demonstrates that designing technological solutions for specific sectors requires a deep understanding of the operational, regulatory, and cultural context of the end user. A CRM for independent pharmacies is not simply a generic CRM adapted for the purpose: it is a tool that must natively incorporate pharmaceutical business logic (expiration control, lot traceability, therapeutic adherence).
The following are recommended for future research: (1) implement and validate the prototype in at least one independent Colombian pharmacy through a longitudinal case study; (2) explore the integration of the system with government health platforms such as SISPRO and RIPS; and (3) analyze sustainable monetization models that ensure the softwareâ€™s accessibility for pharmaceutical micro-enterprises.
